# Scene families and the native gallery

The scene picker now shows one family at a time. Six compact tabs replace the
need to fit every scene into one grid. The current families fit at the existing
215 px panel minimum, using two-column tiles with a 24 px height floor. The
sidebar and preview do not have to grow to accommodate another scene.

Family navigation changes only what is listed. Clicking a tile uses the existing
scene-selection command, including its selected-segment behavior while a scene
plan is enabled. A new active scene from the keyboard, a project or the running
plan reveals its family. The header names the active scene even while browsing
another family. Longer future families scroll in a clipped viewport; clipped
pixels cannot take a press. Tooltips explain each scene's look and response.

Import image and Clear share one footer row; Clear appears when an imported image
exists. The row reserves the same height in either state, so importing does not
move the grid. Import keeps its longer label and most of the row; Clear has a
short label and its own hit target. Its empty seat remains inert when Clear is
absent; Import keeps a fixed width rather than growing underneath it. The
existing empty-Clear negative control caught that regression during integration.

The pure family/layout model is in `core::ui::scene_browser`; the shell draws it.
Families do not reorder `SceneId::ALL`, change keyboard cycling, or enter `.musi`.
`--vocabulary` now includes each scene's family. This is navigation metadata,
separate from the persisted scene token.

Each has eight ordinary Tune controls, routes, presets and section tuning. Music
response zero leaves an intentional ambient picture. Core state owns seeded
clocks and followers; drawing consumes it without advancing it. Still and clip
replay therefore work without intermediate drawing. Pause freezes the followers
and the clock. Live seeking follows the existing discontinuity policy, retaining
scene history; offline replay reconstructs it from the beginning.

Portrait uses short-axis coordinates rather than stretching the landscape image.
The full scene/setting vocabulary is generated by the binary; the bound tables
in [PHASE0_INVENTORY](PHASE0_INVENTORY.md) document the persistence contract.

All previous ids and tokens stay fixed, including Fluid Ink at 15. The six new
rows are appended. Threshold's `beyond` maximum deliberately grows from 16 to 22,
so every new scene can also live in its doorway; 15 still refuses Threshold
self-nesting. Both post-legacy expectation files pin the added controls and
mapping rows. The project and treatment document structures are unchanged.

A seventh family needed a seventh tab without costing tile height. The tabs
stay in two rows, four over three, and each tab is as wide as its label plus a
fixed allowance (`scene_browser::tab_slot`). The shell fits one font size over
those widths, so the longest label in a row is no longer the one squeezed. The
tab strip keeps its height, so every family's tiles keep the viewport they had.
Six scenes is also the most a family can show without scrolling at the 215 px
panel minimum, and that set the count. `scene_gallery_check.py` now expects
seven families. It finds Materials by computing where the label-sized tabs put
it, and it clicks all six Pixel cats tiles at the minimum.

Threshold's `beyond` maximum grows again, from 22 to 28, so the doorway can
show a pixel scene. Both post-legacy expectation files gain only the new rows.

## Verification

Run `tools/scene_gallery_check.py`. It uses a synthetic sustained multiband phrase
with a breathing envelope, including bass and treble at the photographed moment.
A simple rising sweep was insufficient here: by four seconds its bass energy had
already left, so it could not meaningfully test bass-driven mass at that time.
The fixture is audible PCM; the headless wrapper mutes only playback output.

For every new scene the gate checks musical influence, motion, opaque stills,
portrait structure, identical project-reopen pixels, and still/full-video/clip
agreement. It uses High quality (2× supersampling) and probes NVENC with an actual
256×256 encode before selecting it. A 64×64 probe is below this encoder's minimum
frame dimensions and would incorrectly imply that the hardware was unavailable.

The gate also clicks every registered scene tile at 960×640 with a 240 px sidebar,
checks each family's complete membership and unchanged active scene while
browsing, and clicks a category through the real hit-claim path. Core tests cover
future 100-entry families, bounded scrolling, reveal behavior and unique grouping.

`--negative-control` sets music response to zero in both compared treatments; its
music comparison must fail with MAD zero. This verifies the detector through
production settings and rendering, without leaving a source perturbation behind.

The encoder is lossy YUV 4:2:0. Iridescent Pool's fine colour contours measured
31.94 dB against a Balanced-quality PNG; an unencoded RGB→4:2:0→RGB round trip
already measured 35.12 dB. High quality raises the comparison while project
reopen remains pixel-identical. The gate preserves a fixed codec tolerance and
records both still/video and full/clip scores in `measurements.json`; it does not
call a compressed frame pixel-identical. After the 2026-10-24 rework the Pool's
oil bands sit below the 33 dB floor even at High (31.0 dB NVENC, 32.0 dB x264)
while luma measures 39.7 dB and reopen stays identical; see
[Materials](MATERIAL_SCENES.md#verification-2026-10-24).

An independent Sol review caught negative bases in GLSL `pow` calls in Chrome,
Pool and Tunnel. Even squared exponents require nonnegative bases in GLSL;
the affected expressions now apply `abs` first. Chrome grain and Pool's shared
noise lattice use integer hashes, following the repository's driver-consistency
rule. Render observations belong to the dated evidence, not a portable frame-rate
guarantee. The operator's aesthetic judgment remains open.

Artifacts: `build/scene-gallery-check/`, `build/scene-wave/`, plus the three
sibling evidence directories linked in their scene documents. The integrated
gallery is part of `tools/verify.sh`'s full gate. The required release build is
the final deliverable.

The delivered release was also exercised on a real track for 120 frames per new
scene. Every run consumed real decoded PCM with zero dropped samples and zero
output underruns; no frame exceeded the 25 ms budget in these six runs. Logs and
screenshots are `build/scene-wave/*-live.*`. The corrected Chrome/Pool/Tunnel
shaders were rendered on NVIDIA and Mesa CPU as well; their comparisons measured
62.43–63.51 dB, with RGB mean absolute differences below 0.04. This is evidence on
this machine and these passages, not a universal timing or pixel guarantee.

Final integration evidence, 2026-10-23: the quick verification gate passed all
eight stages (1,882 tests passed, one ignored), as did the complete gallery gate,
the fresh 22-tile picker run, and the expected-failure response negative control.
The broad headless sweep completed its other sections but failed the older Knot
picker fixture, which still clicked its former flat-grid position. That fixture
now opens Geometry and clicks Knot's new tile; a fresh complete Knot gate passed
in `build/scene-wave/knot-complete/`. The broad sweep itself was not rerun after
that fixture-only correction. The older Import/Clear probes were also updated
for their shared footer row; their assertions passed in the broad sweep.
